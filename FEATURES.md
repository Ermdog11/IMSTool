# IMSTool — Feature Log

Running record of what the product does and what's planned, kept for business-plan
and marketing material. Plain-language, benefit-first. Updated as we build.

**One-line pitch:** An AI newsroom assistant for team-beat and local-news publishers —
it watches every free source for news on your beat, rates and organizes it, and helps
your writers turn it into publish-ready copy without leaving the tool.

Two product surfaces, one login:
1. **News Monitor** — for the editor. Aggregates and rates everything happening on your beat.
2. **Content Editor** — for every writer. Turns a rough draft into a house-style, publish-ready article.

## Shipped

### News Monitor

| Feature | What it does for the customer |
|---|---|
| All-source aggregation | Pulls from ~60 free feeds — Google News, Bing News, Reddit, team/beat blogs, official athletics site, regional papers, national outlets — with no paid API costs passed on. |
| Sites search engines miss | A small outlet that Google News and Bing don't index, or whose feed address changed, can be read straight from its own site: CoPublisher finds the feed the site advertises, tries the usual feed addresses, or reads the latest headlines off its home page. |
| Phone app (Android and iPhone) | Install CoPublisher on your phone's home screen like an app (full screen, its own icon, shortcuts to Alerts, Write, Chat and Calendar). On a phone the News Monitor gets a thumb-friendly bottom bar (Alerts, Chat, Calendar, Write, More), alert cards with big Write it / Story angles buttons, and no sideways scrolling; the Content Editor's tabs swipe in one row. Breaking news and roster alerts arrive as phone notifications (on iPhone after adding it to the home screen) and open the story when tapped. The computer layout is unchanged. |
| Low-cost scanning | Opening the News Monitor never spends on a new scan: it shows the newsroom's latest saved scan instantly. Fresh scans run on a schedule three times a day (morning, midday, evening) and whenever someone taps **Scan now**; open tabs pick up the newest saved scan on their own. Every story's real publish date is checked, and anything older than two weeks is dropped, so re-indexed old articles never show up as news. |
| Watch-list-driven coverage | Tracks ~250 named people (players, recruits, targets, coaches, staff, alumni, beat reporters) — not just the team name — so niche news isn't missed. |
| AI rating & triage | One pass rates every story 1–5, assigns a category and sport, flags irrelevant items, recycled/republished stories, and stories that need a deeper read. |
| Name-collision handling | Knows the difference between your alum and an unrelated athlete with the same name. |
| Deep-read pass | For ambiguous stories, the tool opens the actual article and re-rates from the full text (used in the digest). |
| Podcasts | Finds recent podcast episodes mentioning your beat, and scans inside national-show episodes for buried mentions. An episode that breaks news or is a real interview with a key figure is rated 4-5 and also shows up in the main Alerts feed and the digest. |
| YouTube | Surfaces relevant videos, rated the same way as articles. Searches are built from the newsroom's beat profile (team names, current people, coaches, alumni, press-conference/podcast/interview and recruiting angles), and a video only counts when its own title is about the team or its people, so opponents' shows and conference roundups that mention the team in passing stay out. Follows up to 60 channels that cover the beat (the ones listed in setup, plus channels it learns post beat videos regularly) through their free upload feeds, which finds small podcasts and interview channels that searches miss; those skip the subscriber floor. Leans into what's hot: whoever the latest scans cover most right now (e.g. the head coach during a job-status story) gets a search slot every refresh, and their videos are listed first with a "Hot topic" tag. Filters out AI-spam, content-farm, gaming and cannabis ("terps") videos. |
| Bluesky | Monitors social chatter, sorted by engagement. Off the header (2026-10-06: too little traffic to earn a tab); still at `/bluesky` for anyone who bookmarked it. |
| Digest emails | Nightly digest and rolling updates, organized by day / sport / rating. Each person picks when they come: the nightly digest at the hour they choose, and 1, 2 or 3 updates a day at their own hours, each covering everything since their last digest email. |
| Breaking-news email | Rating-5 stories trigger an immediate push + email. The scan itself runs 3x/day server-side (more often when the dashboard is open in a browser tab). |
| Breaking-news auto-draft | Every rating-5 story is auto-drafted into a short, hotlinked, publish-ready article the moment it's caught — lands in the Content Editor's Drafts list, emails whoever has breaking alerts on with a direct link to open and finish it, and drops into Team Chat tagged "Breaking News Alert". |
| Two-source check | Before a breaking auto-draft goes out, CoPublisher AI looks for other outlets reporting the same event (the scan's own stories plus a fresh news search on the names in the headline). The email, push and Team Chat post say **Confirmed by On3, The Athletic** or **Single source, unconfirmed**, and the email lists any fact in the draft that other outlets contradict (a position, school, star rating) or don't back up; contradictions also land in the draft's "facts to check" in the Content Editor. If the search shows the event was reported long ago, nothing is sent. Covers the 3x/day digest and the 30-min X/Twitter scan. |
| Roster-change watch | Checks official pages 3×/week and emails when something changes — often before the move is reported anywhere. Three kinds of page: each team's **player roster** (a name added or dropped), each team's **coaches and staff page** (a name added, dropped, or given a new title), and the **athletic department staff directory** for a college beat or the **front office** page for a pro beat. Changes involving a head coach, general manager, athletic director, coordinator or president are starred and listed first. The department/front office check runs and emails separately from the team check. Every newsroom chooses its own pages in setup (the "Roster watch" step), and pages on any site layout work, not just one template. Dedicated in-app view shows who is listed today and a dated history of every change caught. |
| Write it | Every story card has a **Write it** button: drafts a short, hotlinked article from that story in house style and opens it in the Content Editor. A story that already has a draft just opens that one. |
| Request records | Stories with a public record behind them get a **Request records** button, ranked by priority: coaching hires, firings, contracts and buyouts first (bold 📄 button), then sponsorship, apparel, media-rights and game/event agreements, other contracts, NCAA investigations, arrests, lawsuits, settlements and audits, then other money and business news (budgets, bonuses, travel costs, ticket revenue, search firms, regents votes, facilities, revealing emails). CoPublisher checks whether the documents are public under FOIA or the state's open-records law (public universities, stadium authorities and other government bodies; for any school, not just yours), drafts a specific request, and addresses it to the right records office: the one in your beat profile, or one it finds on the agency's own website, filled in automatically only when CoPublisher has confirmed the address appears on that page (an unconfirmed one is shown as "possible address, check it" with a one-click Use it; the online portal is shown if there is one). Review and edit it, then send it from CoPublisher under your name (you're CC'd and replies come to you; editors and publishers), open it in your own email, or copy it. **Suggestions come to you too:** every 2 hours, new priority stories (coach moves, deals and contracts, investigations, arrests and lawsuits, rated 3+) get a ready-to-send draft emailed to editors and publishers with a one-click "Send it from my email" button. Each one also shows up on the Alerts page as "📄 N new suggested records requests" (open it, edit, send, or mark it sent / not needed) and goes in that day's next update email, or the next day's if the last one has gone out. Nothing goes to a records office without a person clicking Send. **Public records requests** alert: CoPublisher finds stories worth a request, writes the letter, and asks if you want it to send it for you (under your name, replies to you) or send it yourself. It knows student privacy law (FERPA) and the standard exemptions and rules, so it never asks for what would be denied on sight (a student's grades, eligibility, injuries or NIL deals; a recruit's file; personnel evaluations; open investigations) and asks for the releasable records instead. Each draft shows its chance of being granted and what it left out on purpose, and only requests likely to be granted are suggested on their own. |
| Story claims | **Claim** on any story card marks it as yours for the whole newsroom: everyone's copy of the card shows "📌 Sam is on it · 2h", the button reads **Taken by Sam** for others and **Release** for you, the claim survives reloads and other browsers, and Team Chat gets a line. A **📌 Claimed** filter shows who's on what. Claims clear after 4 days; editors and publishers can release someone else's. |
| Coverage Desk agent | Runs on its own every morning and emails an assistant-editor's memo: a "📅 Today on the calendar" box with the day's events (games, pressers, deadlines) at the top, what to cover today (with those events factored in), gaps competitors have filled that you haven't, developing threads to follow up, yesterday's numbers (site traffic from Chartbeat and social from Buffer, with a "Yesterday at a glance" box, what worked, any top story that got no social push, and 1-2 actions for today), and an editor's read. Also runnable on demand. |
| Noise controls | Per-topic caps with an overflow view (demote, don't drop), low-priority-sport suppression, stat-page filtering (also old highlight clips, box scores and player pages that search engines show as new), "block this source" list. |
| Own-outlet exclusion | Never shows the customer their own site's stories back to them — publisher lists their own domain(s), plus an "Exclude sources" control to add any other site or URL to filter out. |
| Fact-grounded writing | Every tool that writes (Write it, the Content Editor, emailed drafts, From social, card actions, story ideas, the Ombudsman, the Coverage Desk) works from the newsroom's own knowledge base, current roster and staff, roster changes, beat people and calendar, can search the archive and the web, and is told its own memory is out of date and that old coverage describes the past. Email a press release with instructions and it's written up as a story; email your own finished article and it's saved as sent. Older AI drafts can be rewritten with current facts in one tap. |
| Ask CoPublisher | A question box on Home ("Anything on the calendar this week?", "How are we doing this week?") answered from your own calendar, hot spots, latest news, drafts, analytics, coverage and roster. |
| Advertising tab | Ad slots and where they go, affiliate programs, promos, and this week's hot spots for timing sponsored pieces. |
| Gameplan tab | The film room: one-tap social media checks, the cross-channel strategy read, questions about your audience, and the Ombudsman's coverage review and story ideas. |
| Your own layout | On Home and Gameplan, tap ✏️ Customize to move cards up or down or remove the ones you don't use; your layout follows you to every device and can be reset.  On Home you can also move the search bar and add or remove the individual shortcut tools. |
| Home screen | Where the app opens: search the latest news, the top stories with Scan now, the Content Editor's shortcuts and your latest drafts, the newest team chat, what's coming up on the calendar (including hot spots), advertising and audience (ad slots, analytics, hot social, leaderboard) and shortcuts to the rest of your beat. Shows only what your role can use. |
| Team chat | Real shared channel (persisted, not just your own browser) — message the team, high-rated alerts and auto-drafted breaking stories drop in automatically. Looks like Slack (photo, name, time on every message; anyone can upload their photo), with the newest message at the top under the message box, grouped by day. The publisher can select and delete messages in bulk. |
| Story backlog | Auto-generates story ideas from the current news. Each idea has **Write it** (drafts it and opens it in the Content Editor) and **Fewer like this** (hides it and steers future batches away from similar ideas, for the whole newsroom). |
| Ombudsman | The newsroom's public editor, on the Story backlog tab (⚖️ Ombudsman) and in the morning Coverage Desk memo. **Coverage review:** every morning (or on demand) it reads our articles from the last 3 days and flags possible corrections and fixes: wrong-looking names, titles, numbers and dates, headlines that overpromise, unsourced claims and unexplained anonymous sources, rumor stated as fact, criticism with no response sought, missing context, loaded language, contradictions with our earlier coverage, undisclosed affiliate/sponsor ties. Each flag quotes the words and gives the fix, plus a short column on balance and fairness across the coverage. **Story ideas:** five in every update email (gaps, follow-ups, previews, accountability, reader questions, enterprise), or on demand. |
| Desktop notifications | Optional browser push for breaking news. |

### Content Editor

| Feature | What it does for the customer |
|---|---|
| Copydesk | Paste a writer's draft. Under it, **Add with this edit** checkboxes (latest podcast episode and Related links box, each with a Top-to-Bottom 1-10 spot slider, and promos for this tier) add those in the same pass as the edit, with no extra steps. They start from the newsroom's defaults (set on Permissions & preferences, or by the publisher with **Make these the default** under the draft); unticking or sliding only changes that article; the Podcast, Related links and Promos tabs across the top hold their options (episode position, how many links, which promos). The finished article has **Copy HTML** (the article as HTML code for the CMS's HTML view, so embedded players survive) and each insert has **Copy embed code** for a CMS embed block, since a visual editor strips players on paste. Two modes: **Full edit** rewrites it in house style and the writer's voice with context and links added; **Keep my words** leaves the prose untouched and returns everything else as suggestions. Either way you get reader context (anything unverified flagged), a short list of genuine questions to resolve (trusts the writer on routine facts), related-link suggestions, 3 headline options, and an **SEO panel** (meta description, URL slug, primary + secondary keywords, schema type, and quick fixes for this draft). Now researches while it edits: real web search for a current stat/score/injury status it isn't sure of, and a search over the site's own past coverage (the knowledge base below) to stay consistent with — and link to — what's already been reported, not just a front-page scrape. |
| Per-writer style profiles | Learns each writer's voice from ~15 samples so edits polish rather than flatten them. |
| House style guide | The editable rulebook every edit is measured against — per publication. |
| Related-article links | Automatically hotlinks relevant phrases in the copy to the publication's own recent articles. |
| Related links box | One click adds a **Related** box of 1–4 links to the finished article: your own coverage only, or the best coverage anywhere on the web. You pick where it goes on a 1–10 slider (top to bottom). Real links only, and mostly different from the ones already in the text. |
| Pod-slap | Slap your finished podcast episode into the Content Editor's **🎙️ Pod-slap** tab. CoPublisher listens to it, writes three title options, a description and chapter times from what was said, and puts it on your podcast host as an **unpublished draft** (Buzzsprout, Transistor, Podbean, Megaphone or Spreaker). Nothing goes live: you edit it, save changes to the draft, and publish on your host. On hosts that don't let apps upload drafts (Amperwave, Libsyn, Simplecast and others), you get the title, description and chapters ready to paste. |
| Transcription | Drop in an interview, press conference or podcast (audio or video, up to 1GB), paste a link, or one-click your latest episode. You get a transcript with each speaker labeled. Name the speakers, then copy any quote or drop it straight into your draft. It knows your roster and coaches' names, so it spells them right. **Live mode** transcribes as it plays, from any browser tab (YouTube, a livestreamed press conference, Zoom, Twitch) or your microphone, then turns the recording into the full speaker-labeled version when you stop. **YouTube links:** paste one and the transcript comes straight from the video's own captions, in seconds and at no cost (auto-generated captions are flagged, since they misspell names; there are no speaker labels). Transcripts read as flowing paragraphs of 3–4 sentences, live and finished, with no timestamps, and the speaker's name only when it changes (one click shows times and quote buttons). Live mode keeps recording until you press the **■ Stop** that stays on screen, picking a dropped connection or microphone back up on its own. **Spots to check:** any stretch the transcriber wasn't sure it heard right is listed at the top with its time and highlighted in the text, so you replay it before quoting. **Transcript library:** every transcript is saved automatically for the whole newsroom with an AI-written descriptive title and the date, searchable, and reopens instantly. |
| From social | Paste a social media post (its link, a screenshot, its text, or any mix, plus who posted it and anything you know; X links are read directly, Facebook needs a screenshot) and CoPublisher finds the story for your readers: every current or former player, coach, staff member or recruit with ties to your program, checked against your watch lists. It drafts the article around that angle in your house style, attributes the post, and lists every tie it couldn't confirm for you to check. The draft lands in Drafts, ready to edit. Example: a "top 10 all-time county players" graphic becomes "Two former Terps land on the county's all-time top 10." |
| Drafts manager & email-in | Every draft (saved from Copydesk, emailed in, a breaking auto-draft, Write it, From social) in one searchable list: search headlines, filter by writer, status and source, 25 per page, newest first. **Quick edits right in the row**: rename the headline, change the status, one-click delete with Undo, select several to delete at once, duplicate, or **Edit** to load it into Copydesk. Click a headline to open the full draft (edit the HTML, send to the publisher, copy). **Email an article in**: send it from your team email to the newsroom's +drafts address (paste it in or attach a Word file) and it lands in Drafts within 5 minutes, with a reply linking to it; only team members' verified emails are accepted. |
| "Someone's writing" alert | When someone starts a new article (first **Save draft** in Copydesk, **Write it** on a story card, or **From social**), everyone with **A writer starts an article** switched on (the publisher, by default) gets "✍️ Sam is writing: <headline>" by email or text, with what it was written from and a link straight to the draft. Only the first save alerts, never later edits; the person who started it isn't alerted; each person's days and hours apply. |
| Extra fact-check | An opt-in deeper check (a checkbox wherever it's useful): every checkable claim in the article (names, positions, schools, star ratings, stats, scores, dates, transactions, who said a quote) is checked against live web sources and the outlet's own past coverage, and comes back **Conflict** (with what the source says instead), **Unverified** or **Confirmed**, with the source linked. Opinion and analysis are left alone. Offered as **Extra fact-check** under the draft in Copydesk (runs after the edit; newsroom default set on Permissions & preferences), a **Run it** button on any edit result and any open draft (emailed-in, Write it, breaking auto-drafts), and a checkbox in From social. Conflicts are added to the draft's "Verify before publishing" list. The publisher can switch it off per role. |
| Ad slots (monetization) | Optional ad code in every article. The publisher pastes ad units once (Google AdSense or Ad Manager, Raptive, Mediavine, any network's tag) on the Content Editor's Inserts tab, with the tier and where they go: after paragraph N, then every M paragraphs, at most K per article. With **💰 Ad slots** ticked under the draft (newsroom default on Permissions & preferences), they're placed in the same pass as the edit. The preview shows a labeled box; Copy HTML, Copy embed code and saved drafts carry the real code. A **Top-to-Bottom slider** sets where the first ad goes (promos and affiliate partner boxes have the same slider, also on the Advertising tab): a newsroom default on Permissions & preferences, and one under each draft for that article. |
| Links readers notice | Every link CoPublisher adds to a story (hotlinks, the Related box, the podcast link) is bold, underlined and in the newsroom's link color (Blue by default; Red, Green, any color, or the site's own), set on Permissions & preferences, so links stand out on any site, light or dark. |
| Affiliate revenue share | CoPublisher AI's own affiliate programs (team gear, tickets, sportsbooks where legal) earn newsrooms money. With **💸 Affiliate links** ticked under the draft, links in the article to a program's sites go through CoPublisher's tracked link (tagged with the newsroom as the network's sub-ID), and a program's ready-made box ("Shop Terps gear") can be placed like an ad slot. Every click is counted by newsroom, program and article. On **/affiliates** CoPublisher sets up the programs and each newsroom's share, enters each month's earnings from the networks' reports, and gets a payout report per newsroom (CSV). |
| Inserts & media | Publisher defines reusable blocks once (newsletter sign-up, VIP pitch, related-coverage boxes, ad units) with a tier and a default position. On the editing page the writer or editor clicks to add one — it drops in at its default spot, overridable per article, previewed live. Plus **one-click embed of your latest podcast episode** (or pick an older one), in your podcast platform's own player format: Amperwave, Megaphone, Buzzsprout, Libsyn, Spreaker, Simplecast, Transistor or any other host. Paste one sample embed code once and every episode matches it. |
| Give directions / ask a question | A chat box available both while writing (on the Copydesk compose screen, before running an edit) and on the result after an edit: ask for a change ("tighten the second paragraph", "cut the last line") or a question ("what's the source for the visit date?", "what's Malik Washington's rushing total this season?", "have we covered this before?") — it revises the draft/article in place or answers, searching the web for current info or the site's own archive for prior coverage, whichever the question needs, like talking to Claude about a draft. The publisher can optionally connect their own 247Sports login (one-time, in Settings) so this reads the full paywalled article instead of just the free preview. **The archive is now searchable, not just stored** — Copydesk and Ask the editor can both search it directly (full-text) to check prior coverage, stay consistent, and find a real internal link beyond the front-page scrape. ## Roadmap

### Near term
- **Publish connector** — file a finished article straight into the customer's CMS as a draft (never auto-live). One-click for WordPress and Ghost; clean formatted export for Substack, Squarespace, and closed CMSes.
- **Per-writer content catalog** — a complete index of everything each writer has published, feeding a fuller style model and the internal-linking. (Builds on the knowledge base.)
- **Knowledge-base ownership** — each newsroom decides, per writer, whether the knowledge built from that writer's work belongs to the publication, to the writer (portable — they can export it and take it with them), or both. Set up front.
- **Deeper related-links** — internal links drawn from the full archive, not just the front page, plus a writer-driven "pick one of 3 suggested links" control.
- **Photo upload tool** — writers attach images to a draft in the Content Editor.
- **Video auto-suggest** — publisher configures where their videos live (YouTube channel / Vimeo / feed); the Content Editor drops a relevant video into the draft.
- **Faster scans** — reduce scan time without cutting coverage.

### Mid term
- **Text with CoPublisher (key future feature, Jeff 2026-10-06)** — a two-way text conversation with CoPublisher as a newsroom assistant: it keeps track of what's going on and organizes it, sends reminders, asks what needs to be done, and texts you when something needs you now (a social post going viral, breaking news, a competitor scoop). You can text back to act on it ("write it", "remind me at 3", "what's on today?") or text in an article to become a draft. Each person sets how much they hear from it (everything, important only, a daily summary, quiet hours) in Permissions & preferences.
- **Article templates** — pick a structure (game recap, transfer-portal piece, positional ranking, interview feature), paste raw notes, get an ~80%-done draft fast.
- **Archive fact-checker** — before publishing, cross-references claims in a draft against the newsroom's own past coverage to catch contradictions. Builds on the knowledge base.
- **Assignment board** — claiming is shipped (see Story claims); still to come: a board view of everyone's open claims across scans, with status (writing / filed / published).
- **Writer research suite** — a scouting-report generator (team + week → depth chart, roster, results, stats, matchup notes), a one-query stat lookup (career stats, game logs, comparisons), postgame quote transcripts, and a searchable historical-records database — everything a writer would otherwise dig up by hand across ESPN, the school site, and memory, in one place.
- **Social post generator, standalone** — turn any finished article into X/Facebook/Instagram posts plus a thread version, usable on its own instead of buried in a dashboard action; sending straight to Buffer is a stretch goal.
- **Reader newsletter, fully managed** — an auto-built daily/weekly newsletter from published articles and trending topics, with IMSTool managing the subscriber list and sending it — not just a draft handoff. A sellable option for publishers who don't already run a newsletter platform.
- **Invoicing** — writers and contractors submit their monthly invoices through the app; the publisher marks each one paid, and paid records are kept for taxes with a year-end per-payee export for 1099s. Records and tracking, not a payment processor.
- **Audience analytics** — connect Google Analytics, Chartbeat, Parse.ly, Search Console, and your Facebook/Instagram page. Get real-time alerts when a story is taking off or dying, a weekly read on what's working (best day and time to publish, which topics and headline styles land, which evergreens are slipping), proactive suggestions, and a question box: *"What's been working best for us this month?"* — answered with specifics, because it knows what each piece was about and who wrote it.
- **Writer analytics** — per-writer views and engaged time on page, broken out by the kind of story (recruiting, recap, feature…), with loose nudges like *"your last two commitment pieces averaged 38% more views than your baseline."* A fair, data-backed read for the publisher; their own dashboard for the writer.
- **Multi-tenant onboarding** — a new publisher self-configures their beat (watch list, feeds, house style) via a guided, AI-assisted wizard in minutes — no code changes.
- **Scheduled agents** — proactive assistants that run on a cadence and push results: competitive-coverage scan, coverage-gap audit, evergreen-refresh finder, recruiting-board watch.
- **Shared newsroom settings** — style guide, writer profiles, and block lists shared across the team instead of per-browser. (Writer profiles and promos: done 2026-10-07.)

## Positioning notes

- **Two moats.** (1) The free-source aggregation — real-time beat coverage out of free
  feeds and search operators, no $200/mo social API, no per-seat news-API fees. (2) The
  newsroom knowledge base — the longer a customer uses it, the more it knows their beat
  and their writers, and the harder it is to leave.
- **Knowledge-base ownership may be worth more than it looks.** Once every article and
  every writer's voice model is captured and portable, that corpus is an asset in its own
  right: a career asset a writer carries between outlets, a retention lever and an
  acquisition line-item for a publication, and a reason for IMSTool to be the neutral
  place it lives. The near-term build is a settings toggle; design it deliberately.
- **The Content Editor is likely the stronger recurring-revenue hook** (per-writer-seat,
  used daily) vs. the Monitor (per-newsroom).
- **Human stays in control** everywhere it matters: nothing publishes itself, alerts are
  opt-in per person, AI-added context is flagged for verification.
- Built vertical-agnostic under the hood — Maryland Terrapins is the first beat, not a
  hardcoded assumption.
